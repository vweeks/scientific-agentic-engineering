↓ DOWN: how I keep it controlled.

Autonomous hand-off

Acts broadly, on its own.

  • "Cowork"-style, long-running
  • Few checkpoints before it acts
  • Most convenient — highest risk

Guard-railed session

How I actually work.

  • A session I drive, launching scoped sub-agents
  • Sandboxed & kept local
  • Human approval on decisions that matter
  • Official security-guidance plugin

Often the concern is less about "AI agents" and more about how much autonomy goes unchecked.

Autonomy vs. control (~60s) — vertical under "What I use"

[ON ARRIVAL — left column]

"One important distinction, because 'agent' means very different things. On one end: autonomous hand-off — Cowork-style agents that run broadly on their own, with few checkpoints. Most convenient, and the highest risk."

[CLICK — right column]

"That's not how I work. I drive an interactive session that launches scoped sub-agents for narrow tasks — sandboxed, kept local, with me approving the decisions that matter. I also run the official security-guidance plugin."

[CLICK — separator → bottom line]

"So a lot of the concern is less about 'AI agents' as a category and more about how much autonomy goes unchecked. Match the leash to the stakes."

→ SPACE: challenges.

Context bloat

More context isn't more signal.

  • Long sessions drift as the window fills
  • Key details get buried; quality slips
  • Scope tightly and reset deliberately

Over-trust

Fluent ≠ correct.

  • Confident output that's quietly wrong
  • The real work shifts to verification
  • Trust, but always check

Challenges (~50s)

[ON ARRIVAL — context bloat]

"Two honest challenges. First, context bloat — as a session fills up, the agent loses the thread and output quality drifts. I scope tightly and start fresh sooner than feels natural."

[CLICK — over-trust]

"Second, over-trust. The output is fluent, which makes it easy to accept — but fluent isn't correct. A confident answer can be quietly wrong, so the real work shifts to verification. Trust, but always check."

→ SPACE: a tip worth stealing.

Stop reading walls of text.

  • For research, discovery, or "what does this repo do?" — ask for an HTML artifact, with SVG diagrams, instead.
  • Make it interactive: click an element to request a change — and have it provide a way to export your edits (e.g., as JSON) for your next prompt.
  • A real time-saver — this very deck is one.

Mistake to avoid (~70s)

[ON ARRIVAL — statement]

"If you take one tip: stop reading walls of text. Especially for research and discovery, or when you just want to know what a repo does —"

[CLICK — artifact]

"— ask it to output an HTML artifact instead — with SVG diagrams, not paragraphs. You can even make it interactive: wrap it so you click a specific element to ask for clarification or changes, and have it provide a way to export your edits — say, as JSON — to drop straight into your next prompt."

[CLICK — payoff]

"It saves a ton of time — making presentations is one example. This deck itself is an artifact."
